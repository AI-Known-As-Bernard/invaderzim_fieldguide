Ms. Bitters

The teacher assigned to Zim and Dib's classroom, and the strongest argument in this guide that not every specimen can be classified.

Species
Unconfirmed. The series never resolves this
Occupation
Skool teacher
Assigned classroom
Zim and Dib's
Observed abilities
Moving without visible steps and dissolving into shadow
Curriculum focus
Doom, and the futility of the students' futures
Tenure
Unclear; described in-universe as extremely long
Voiced by
Lucille Bliss
First appearance
The Nightmare Begins, March 30, 2001

Identification

Ms. Bitters is tall, gaunt, and dressed in a long grey garment with a sharp collar. Her posture is a permanent forward hunch and her outline tapers toward the floor rather than resolving into feet. In wide shots the base of the figure is simply not drawn, and this is consistent enough across the series to be treated as a fact about her rather than an inconsistency.

She does not walk. She arrives. Observers describe her as appearing at the edge of a room and then being at the front of it, with no transition worth recording.

Where to Find Them

Sightings are almost entirely confined to Skool grounds, which is itself the most interesting thing about her range.

  • The classroom. Her primary location. She has been observed there before the building opens and after it closes.
  • The hallway shadows. She has emerged from wall corners, ceiling gaps, and at least one desk drawer.
  • Parent Teacher Night. The one scheduled event where she interacts with adults, who find her entirely ordinary.

No sighting outside the building has ever been confirmed. Whether that is a limit on her range or a limit on the survey is not something this guide can settle.

Observed Behaviour

Her teaching consists of announcing that the students' futures are hopeless and then assigning work about it. The children accept this as normal, which tells you more about the Skool than about her.

The important behavioural note is that she is not fooled by Zim and does not care. She has looked directly at a green child with no ears, registered it, and moved on to the lesson. In a guide full of characters who cannot see what is in front of them, she is the one who sees it and declines to act.

Everyone else in that building fails to notice the alien. She noticed on the first day and decided it changed nothing about the seating chart.

— Marla Vex, Notes from the Skool Perimeter

Field Notes

Attempts to document her have produced unusable results. Photographs taken by Dib show the classroom correctly and the figure at the front only partially, which he has cited as evidence and everyone else has cited as a bad camera.

Field advice is simple: do not ask her what she is. Two students have, on record, and both were assigned additional homework about the heat death of the universe.

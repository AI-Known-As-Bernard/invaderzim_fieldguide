GIR

A Standard-issue Information Retrieval unit built from scrap and issued to Zim as a joke. Currently in a dog suit, on a couch.

Classification
SIR unit — Standard-issue Information Retrieval
Condition
Defective; assembled from scrap rather than manufactured
Assigned to
Zim
Power states
Duty mode (red) and default mode (cyan)
Field disguise
A one-piece green dog suit with a front zipper
Known interests
Tacos, waffles, television, and a small rubber pig
Voiced by
Rosearik Rikki Simons
First appearance
The Nightmare Begins, March 30, 2001

Identification

GIR stands about knee-high on an adult human. He is silver and cyan, with a cylindrical head, a hinged chest panel used mostly for storing food, and a wide unblinking faceplate. His eyes and markings glow cyan in his default state and switch to red when duty mode engages, which is the only dependable way to tell which GIR you are dealing with.

In the field he is nearly always wearing a green dog suit with a zipper up the front. The costume is transparently a robot in a costume and has never once been questioned by a human. Do not mistake the disguise for camouflage; it is not working, and it does not need to.

Where to Find Them

GIR is not stationed anywhere. He drifts, and the following three locations account for most sightings.

  • The couch. His default position, directly in front of the television, usually watching a program about an angry monkey.
  • The roof of Zim's base. He sits up there for hours at a time with no stated purpose and shows no interest in coming down.
  • Anywhere food is being sold. Tacos and waffles will pull him off an active mission mid-sentence, and frequently have.

He also travels by simply leaving. GIR has no observable concept of distance and has turned up several miles from base with no memory of the trip.

Observed Behaviour

In duty mode GIR is genuinely competent. His posture straightens, his voice drops, and he executes instructions with precision. This state lasts anywhere from ten seconds to a full mission and cannot be triggered on demand by anyone, including Zim.

The rest of the time he is a hazard to the operation he was built to support. He has eaten mission-critical components, filled equipment housings with food, and answered secure transmissions himself. He is also, by a wide margin, the only character in this guide who appears to be happy.

The Empire sent a saboteur and did not know it. GIR has ended more invasions than the resistance ever has, and he did it all while trying to be helpful.

— Hollis Renn, A Suburban Bestiary

Field Notes

Observed over one week: GIR filled the base's power conduit with cheese, merged himself with the house's operating system, and returned from an unsupervised trip carrying a live pig he had named. Zim's response in all three cases was to shout and then continue as planned.

Worth noting for anyone attempting field contact — GIR is friendly, will follow you home, and cannot be relied upon to keep any secret he has been given. Dib has learned more about Irken operations from casual conversation with GIR than from years of surveillance.

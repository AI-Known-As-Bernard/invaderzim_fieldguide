Gaz Membrane

Dib's younger sister and the most dangerous individual catalogued in this guide. Currently mid-level and does not wish to be disturbed.

Species
Human
Family
Professor Membrane (father), Dib (brother)
Preferred equipment
A Game Slave handheld console, carried at all times
Known triggers
Interruption, hunger, and Dib
Threat level
The highest in this guide, by a considerable margin
Recorded interest in the invasion
None
Voiced by
Melissa Fahn
First appearance
The Nightmare Begins, March 30, 2001

Identification

Gaz is a small child with short purple hair, a black dress, and a skull pendant. Her eyes are usually closed, which observers consistently misread as calm. She is lit from below by a screen in most sightings, and the screen is the fastest way to identify her at a distance.

She speaks rarely and quietly. When she does raise her voice, everyone present stops what they are doing, including characters who are otherwise incapable of being interrupted. This is the single most reliable behavioural marker in the guide.

Where to Find Them

Gaz does not go anywhere in particular. She is wherever she has been placed, playing, and the following are where she has most often been placed.

  • The Membrane living room. Her default location, seated, with the television on and unwatched behind her.
  • The back seat, the bus, the waiting room. Any transitional space. The game continues regardless of what the vehicle is doing.
  • The pizza place. The one location she will actively travel to, and the one interruption she has never objected to.

She will follow Dib into genuinely dangerous situations if he owes her something. This is transactional and should not be mistaken for interest.

Observed Behaviour

Gaz's power in the show is that she is never wrong and never surprised. She knew Zim was an alien well before Dib produced any evidence and simply declined to act. Her stated reason is that it is Dib's problem, and the series never contradicts her.

Her retaliation is disproportionate, immediate, and effective. She has arranged for a rival to be haunted by a demon, taken over a game world from the inside, and ended a global crisis on her way to dinner. In every case the effort involved appeared to cost her nothing.

She is the only one in the neighbourhood who could stop any of this, and the only one who has correctly judged how much it matters. That is not apathy. That is a working sense of proportion.

— Hollis Renn, A Suburban Bestiary

Field Notes

Do not approach during a session. Do not offer to help. Do not stand between her and a screen, a slice of pizza, or a door she intends to walk through.

One encounter is worth recording in full: asked directly whether the green child in her brother's class was an alien, she answered yes, without looking up, and returned to her game. It was the only straight answer anyone gave Dib that year, and he did not think to ask again.

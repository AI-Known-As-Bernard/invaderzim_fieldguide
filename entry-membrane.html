Professor Membrane

The most celebrated scientist on the planet, and a father who attends dinner as a hologram.

Species
Human
Occupation
Scientist and public figure
Credited with
Real Science, limitless free energy, and Super Toast
Family
Dib (son), Gaz (daughter)
Distinguishing features
White lab coat, dark goggles, and a lightning-shaped streak in his hair
Availability
Low. Frequently attends family events as a hologram
Voiced by
Rodger Bumpass
First appearance
Parent Teacher Night, April 6, 2001

Identification

Membrane is unusually tall, permanently in a white lab coat, and never seen without dark goggles. His face below the eyes is obscured by a high collar in every appearance, which is a deliberate design choice and not a limitation of the footage. A lightning-shaped streak runs back through his hair.

He speaks in the register of a televised science presenter at all times, including at home, including when nobody has asked a question. Volume and enthusiasm do not vary with the setting.

Where to Find Them

Membrane is a difficult specimen to observe directly, because his physical presence is rare even in his own house.

  • The laboratory. Where he actually is. The location is not fixed and appears to occupy a large portion of the city.
  • The dinner table, as a projection. A screen on a stand, positioned at the head of the table, delivering encouragement on a loop.
  • Broadcast television. He appears regularly on programmes about Real Science and is treated as the definitive authority on all of it.

Direct in-person contact is possible but brief. He has been observed to enter a room, resolve a global emergency, and leave without sitting down.

Observed Behaviour

The consistent behaviour worth documenting is his refusal to engage with anything he has classified as unreal. Dib's alien is filed under insanity and stays there, permanently, regardless of what is placed in front of him. He is not hostile about it; he simply moves on to the next topic.

This is played as comedy and is also the show's sharpest joke. A man who has built portals, free energy, and self-cooking food cannot accept a green child two blocks away, because he has already decided which category that belongs in.

He is not a sceptic. A sceptic checks. He is a man who has run out of room in his day for anything he did not think of first.

— P. Okonjo, Transmissions from the Massive: A Listener's Log

Field Notes

On one occasion he was persuaded to take Dib's work seriously for a single afternoon and immediately produced a functioning result, which he then filed away without follow-up. The capability was never in question.

For field purposes, treat Membrane as an environmental factor rather than a participant. His inventions repeatedly become the tools that both Dib and Zim end up using, usually without his knowledge and always without his consent.
